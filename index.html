Source layers & chronology
Fayette County GIS lists:
LiDAR / bare-earth DEM: 2007, 2013, 2021.
Digital aerial photography: 1938, 1956, 1964, 1970, 1980, 1985, 1988, 2000.
Orthophotography: 1994 and 2004–2025; oblique imagery includes multiple vintages through 2023.
Also listed: historical drainage/tile, soils, slope, floodplain, cemeteries, roads, bridges/culverts and NGS monuments. The 1938, 1956, 1964, circa-1968 topo, 1980 and 1985 mosaics are wired directly into this atlas as live toggleable layers. The county also lists 2007/2013/2021 LiDAR and bare-earth DEM datasets; I have not found public standalone tile endpoints for those three vintages, so the live relief products here use the current USGS 3DEP elevation service.

How I am assigning probabilities
I am treating this like a working Bayesian field hypothesis rather than a binary “confirmed / not confirmed” label. Geometry visible in more than one relief product raises confidence that the shape is real. Coincidence with known floodplain forms, moraines, road grading or drainage lowers the probability of an ancient constructed origin. Persistence in older aerials before modern modification raises the probability that a feature predates current agriculture/roads. A percentage can and should move when better data arrives.

I will state the conclusion plainly in percentages rather than use a hidden codeword to imply certainty I cannot support.
NO FLUFF analysis notes
A1 circle/arcuate feature: deserves real scrutiny, but aspect coloring can make weak curvature look dramatic. What would move it sharply upward is a continuous rim or paired scarp in hillshade/slope that persists independently of farm rows and modern drainage.

A2 isolated hill: currently my favorite discrimination target. If its profile, soils and toe geometry are unlike neighboring glacial knobs, it becomes significantly more interesting. If it matches the regional hummock/moraine population, natural origin becomes more likely.

B3 low field: potentially important regardless of archaeology because it can explain other shapes. A paleochannel or old floodplain surface can generate arcs, crescents, ridges and crop/soil contrasts that mimic earthworks.
